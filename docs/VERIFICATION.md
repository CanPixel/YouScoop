## Retrieval and archive structure (2026-10-02)

| Change | Measured | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Hybrid search | Embeddings moved from each passage's JSON into a binary column; whole-word, substring (trigram) and annotation matches weighted by term rarity; near-duplicates skipped. On a 22-question benchmark against the actual source: relevant passage in the top 6 for 22 of 22 questions (was 21; the Larry Summers email, named only inside "larrysummers.com", now found), mean reciprocal rank 0.955 (unchanged), near-duplicate pairs in the top 12 from 19 to 0. Reciprocal rank fusion was tried and rejected: it dropped the mean reciprocal rank to 0.865 | Broad questions about a kind of item ("which emails are shown") miss email screens that never say "email"; the answer then says none were shown |
| Search at scale | A synthetic 20,000-passage library: 36 ms per search against 205 ms for parsing every passage, the previous method. Indexing happens at import (about 0.5 ms per passage), so the first question after an import is not slow; existing libraries are indexed once on first search | Android timing at this size is not measured |
| Annotation carry-forward | Reprocessing gives passages new IDs. Notes, judgements and concept links now move to the new passages that cover the same moment and at least 70% of the same words, marked as carried, without overwriting newer annotations; word-for-word corrected readings (Ball to Baal) are re-applied to the new text. With the actual old (112-passage) and new (voice-separated) packages, a note, two corrected readings and the concept links reached the right new passages | Corrections that rewrite a passage rather than swap words stay with the old revision, which remains preserved |
| Follow-up questions | A short follow-up or one that refers back ("who sent it?") also searches with the previous question | |
| Quotation checking | A quotation now needs every word, in order, in a supplied passage; one spelling variant per eight words is allowed for corrected OCR typos. This flags "goyim" rewritten as "globe" and "Bank Name: Baal" | The check cannot tell which passage a correct quote is attributed to |
| Frame descriptions | The SmolVLM2 prompt no longer asks for on-screen text, and descriptions that still report text where OCR read the screen are left out of the index and archive (16 of 60 on the test source, including an invented email sender). Existing videos re-describe their keyframes through Update analysis, 23 seconds for the test source. The Oracle is told frame descriptions are unreliable for wording, and a quote found only in one is labeled as possibly invented | The model still describes some scenes wrongly (a search page as a person at a computer) |

| Screen kinds and listing questions | On-screen passages are labeled by layout (Email, Memo, Wikipedia page, News article, Search results, Government document, Creator's note). On the test source all 14 email screens were labeled correctly and none was missed. A question asking for every item of a kind ("Which emails are shown?") now gets every passage of that kind in time order: the Oracle listed all 14 emails where it previously said none were shown, and listed the Wikipedia pages. Plural questions also match singular words | Listing works for these kinds only; screens without a clear layout marker (assistant answers, handwritten statements) stay unlabeled. YouScoop's own per-video chat does not use listing yet |
| Screen keyframes | Each keyframe the frame model describes is also read by OCR at full resolution. Frames with at least 200 letters of non-banner text (screens measured 426 to 1,231, talking-head frames at most 51) get a factual label such as "The frame shows an email" instead of the model's guess: 6 of 60 on the test source, including the Google search page described as a person at a computer and the invented email sender | The remaining 54 descriptions still come from SmolVLM2 and can be vague or wrong about people and settings |
| Spelling suggestions | Caption words that sound like a capitalized name shown on at least three screens nearby are re-transcribed by Whisper, without and with the on-screen spelling as a hint. On the test source: Moolok to Moloch and Bal to Baal were heard without a hint; Ball to Baal with the hint, where Whisper also kept "ball" in "the vampire's ball"; Moolok to Molek was dropped in favor of Moloch. 19 seconds. Suggestions appear in SACRiNOMiCON's spelling panel and apply only on request | "goyam" for "goyim" is missed because the screens show it in lowercase. A hint can bias Whisper, which is why hint-only suggestions are labeled and show what was heard |

| Listing in YouScoop's chats | The quick chat and the Gemma investigation chat use the same listing rule as the Oracle. Asked "Which emails are shown on screen?", the quick chat listed 13 of the 14 email screens in order with timestamps, with a larger answer limit for lists; Gemma's context includes every passage of the kind first | The 4B quick-chat model skipped the 27:56 Summers email even though it was supplied |
| Frame model | Qwen2.5-VL 3B (Apache-2.0) replaces SmolVLM2 for frame descriptions when installed; it downloads on first use and SmolVLM2 remains the fallback. On 20 hand-checked frames it invented about 3 details against 11 for SmolVLM2 and 9 for Gemma 3 4B. Across all 60 frames of the test source, headphone mentions (he wears none) fell from 17 to 3 and specific correct details rose (the Y cap in 50 frames against 10, the crucifix in 18 against 0). Its image encoder runs on the CPU because the Metal path produced invalid output in this llama.cpp build; 2.3 seconds per frame, about 3 minutes per video | It still attributes the chair's red trim to the jacket, sometimes calls the seated presenter standing, and hedges the silver cross as "possibly a Christmas ornament" |
| Lowercase terms | Spelling candidates no longer need a capitalized on-screen form; Whisper confirmation filters them. "goyam" to "goyim" is now suggested, heard without a hint, and suggestions keep the captions' own letter case so a case-sensitive correction actually applies | |

### First user test on the 32:45 source (2026-10-02)

- The raw Omni moments shown during that test came from a sweep run with a worker copy that predates the first-response stop (the copy in Application Support lacks it; the app rewrites that copy on each Omni run). Without the stop, Omni continued past its answer into invented "Human:" turns and repeated refusals until the length limit. The two later twelve-clip runs with the stop (`omni-batch-first-response.json`, `omni-batch-visual-only.json`) had no refusals in 24 answers, but half repeated the transcript and the rest were generic or wrong.
- Qwen2.5-VL produced no refusals in 60 frames, but 16 descriptions stopped mid-sentence at the 96-token limit. Frame descriptions now get 160 tokens and are cut back to the last whole sentence.
- Keyframes were thinned from 558 scene-change candidates by list position, so busy stretches (a flashing "AVAILABLE ON PATREON" overlay) kept frames one second apart, such as five at 18:53–18:57. Scene changes within 3 seconds of a kept frame are now skipped, and thinning keeps frames spaced evenly in time: on this source the 60 kept frames are 24.7–71.8 seconds apart.
- The 29:12 on-screen text that looks garbled is a faithful reading: the page shown is a handwritten grid of jumbled letters. The 29:18 passage was a partial reading of the page shown in full at 29:32. Screens now also merge when 60% of the smaller reading's words occur in the other one (and it has at least 8 words); on this source that merges three partial/full pairs (61 to 58 screens), while two different neighbouring emails sharing 52% stay separate.
- YouScoop chat, Gemma and the Oracle prompts now say to report disturbing or offensive source material accurately without refusing, softening or moralizing, and that reporting is not endorsing.
- Omni was then removed from YouScoop (inspection panel, sweep, reviewed observations, worker task, model catalog entry and the 11 GB model files). The audiovisual evidence kind stays in the shared archive core so packages saved earlier still open. The pinned Python runtime is unchanged because Gemma's MLX stack also needs it.
- "Update analysis" applies these to saved videos as `onscreen:3` (regrouping only, no new OCR) and `scenes:4` (new keyframes and descriptions).
